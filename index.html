        State-Level Science Exhibition · Student prototype

        From a shelf of paper files to one organized digital history

        MediTrack 360 shows how a hospital could give authorized doctors a patient's past visits, allergies, medications and laboratory results through a single unique Patient ID, with privacy protections built into every step.

        The problem

        In many hospitals and clinics, a patient's history lives in physical files: handwritten notes, printed lab slips and discharge summaries stored in folders and record rooms.

        Finding a file means searching by name through shelves or registers. Files get misplaced or split across departments, handwriting can be hard to read, and an allergy noted years ago can sit buried on page twelve. When a patient arrives in an emergency or visits a new department, the doctor may not see that history in time.

        How MediTrack 360 helps

        Every patient gets a unique Patient ID. An authorized doctor enters the ID, or scans the patient's QR card, and sees one organized profile.

        • Documented allergies are shown first in a red alert card
        • Visits, investigations and procedures in one chronological timeline
        • Lab results charted against reference ranges and comparable across dates
        • Medication records shown exactly as documented by doctors
        • Registration generates a unique Patient ID with contact and emergency contact details
        • An emergency summary puts blood group, allergies, current medicines and the emergency contact on one screen
        • An audit log records who opened which record, and when

        Finding a recordSearch shelves or registers by name; names can repeatEnter one unique Patient ID or scan a QR card
        Allergy visibilityDepends on someone reading every pageShown first, highlighted, on every screen for that patient
        Comparing lab resultsFlip through separate printed slipsTrend chart and side-by-side comparison of any two dates
        Who looked at the fileUsually not recordedEvery record opening is written to an access log
        Access controlAnyone with the folder can read itSign-in, role-based permissions and PIN confirmation

        Privacy and safety by design

        • Only signed-in staff can open records; the front-desk role sees identity and allergies but not clinical details
        • QR codes contain only the Patient ID, so a lost card reveals no medical information
        • Opening a record from a QR code requires the user's PIN
        • Three wrong PINs or 10 minutes of inactivity lock the session
        • The system never generates diagnoses or recommends medicines; it only displays what was documented

        Limitations of this prototype

        • All patients, doctors and results are fictional and built into the page
        • Sign-in runs in the browser to demonstrate the workflow. A real deployment needs server-side authentication, encrypted storage and audited access
        • New registrations and the audit log are kept only in the open browser tab and clear on reload, so no personal data is stored
        • Not connected to any hospital database and not clinically validated

        Future scope

        A full version could store records on a secure hospital server with encryption, use two-factor sign-in for staff, exchange data with other hospitals through health-data standards such as HL7 FHIR, and let patients view their own records with consent controls.
